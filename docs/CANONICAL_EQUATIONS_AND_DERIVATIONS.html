Relativistic Time Disentanglement: Proper Time ($\tau$) vs. Metric Coordinate Time ($t$)

A critical foundation of Chronodynamic Relativity is the rigorous separation between observer coordinate time $t$ and worldline proper time $\tau$. Along any physical worldline, the differential line element establishes: $$\mathrm{d}\tau = \eta_{\text{dyn}}(t, v) \, \mathrm{d}t = \frac{1}{\gamma_{\text{dyn}}(t)} \, \mathrm{d}t$$

Consequently, the frame conversion operator between proper time derivatives and coordinate time derivatives is: $$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau} \equiv \frac{1}{\eta_{\text{dyn}}} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \gamma_{\text{dyn}} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}$$

This operator prevents any derivative skew between laboratory frame tracking (where coordinate velocity $v = \mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}t$ and 3-force $F = \mathrm{d}p/\mathrm{d}t$ reside) and particle worldlines (where 4-acceleration $a^\mu = \mathrm{d}u^\mu/\mathrm{d}\tau$ and atomic clock frequencies reside).
