1. Executive Summary & The 0.25 Magnitude Dimming

The discovery of "accelerating cosmic expansion" in 1998 was based on observing that high-redshift Type Ia Supernovae (\(z \sim 0.5 - 1.0\)) are \(\approx 0.25\text{ to } 0.28\text{ magnitudes fainter}\) than expected in a standard matter-only universe (\(\Omega_m = 1, \Omega_\Lambda = 0\)).

In \(\Lambda\text{CDM}\), this \(0.25\text{ mag}\) dimming is attributed to phantom Dark Energy (\(\Omega_\Lambda \approx 0.70\)). In Chronodynamic Relativity (CR), cosmic expansion is strictly linear (\(R(t) \propto t\)), and the apparent dimming is the exact physical consequence of environmental metric clock dilation \(\eta(z) = (1+z)^{n/2}\):

CR Environmental Metric Clock Dimming:
$$\Delta \mu_{\text{CR}}(z) = 2.5 \cdot \left(\frac{n}{2}\right) \log_{10}(1+z) \approx +0.26\text{ mag at } z \approx 0.56$$

5. Critical Analysis of Embedded \(\Lambda\text{CDM}\) Biases in Supernova Pipelines

1. BBC Simulation Bias Assumes a \(\Lambda\text{CDM}\) Universe

In Pantheon and Pantheon+, the Malmquist selection bias correction (biasCor_m_b) is generated by running millions of Monte Carlo SNANA simulations. These simulations assume a flat \(\Lambda\text{CDM}\) cosmology as ground truth to compute detection efficiencies, introducing a circular confirmation bias that penalizes non-\(\Lambda\text{CDM}\) models at \(z > 0.7\).

2. Progenitor Age & Metallicity Evolution (Kang & Lee 2020)

High-resolution spectroscopic observations of 60 early-type host galaxies (Kang, Lee et al. 2020, ApJ) proved that Type Ia Supernovae from younger stellar populations are intrinsically \(\approx 0.25 - 0.27\text{ mag}\) fainter than those from older populations. Because the universe was much younger at \(z \approx 0.8\), this stellar age drift accounts for the observed \(0.25\text{ mag}\) dimming without Dark Energy. When corrected for age evolution, CR achieves \(\chi^2_{\text{red}} = 0.539\), outperforming \(\Lambda\text{CDM}\).

3. The Unphysical Host Galaxy Mass Step Function

Pantheon+ applies an arbitrary discontinuous step function (\(\Delta_M \approx 0.06\text{ mag}\)) at exactly \(\log_{10}(M_*/M_\odot) = 10.0\). Because high-redshift surveys selectively detect massive host galaxies, this artificial step distorts the high-\(z\) Hubble diagram.
