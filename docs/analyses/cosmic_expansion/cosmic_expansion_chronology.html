This report documents the detailed mathematical and physical relationships of cosmic expansion, clock rate variations, and distance measures under Chronodynamic Relativity (CR). We show how observed cosmic acceleration and redshift profiles emerge naturally as clock-shifting coordinate artifacts of a linearly expanding universe with a constantly diluting space-density field.

1. Fundamental Equations & Physical Postulates

A. Metric Clock Coupling

The tick rate of local proper clocks ($d\tau$) relative to coordinate time ($dt$) is determined by the local space-density field $\rho_s$:

$$ds^2 = -c^2 \left( \frac{\rho_s}{\rho_0} \right) dt^2 + \gamma_{ij} dx^i dx^j$$ $$\frac{d\tau}{dt} = \sqrt{\frac{\rho_s}{\rho_0}}$$

B. Space Density Dilution

In the standard baseline model of Chronodynamic Relativity, the space density field dilutes continuously at a constant power-law rate as the universe expands:

$$\rho_s(z) = \rho_0 (1+z)^n$$

where $n = 0.239034$ is the global dilution exponent derived from galactic dynamics and supernovae fits. This dilution is driven by the physical expansion of space and continues indefinitely as long as expansion proceeds.

C. Perceived vs. Physical Redshift

Light observed today suffers a dual redshift: physical spatial expansion ($z_{\text{exp}}$) and metric clock dilation ($z_{\text{field}}$):

$$1 + z_{\text{obs}} = (1 + z_{\text{exp}}) \cdot (1 + z_{\text{field}}) = (1 + z_{\text{exp}})^{1 + n/2}$$

2. Local Clock Tick Rate History

We trace the relative tick rate of clocks in the past relative to coordinate time and proper age of the universe. In proper time, the universe has a finite age $\tau_0$ from the Big Bang ($z \to \infty$), where clocks ran continuously faster in the past due to space-density dilution:

$$\tau(z_{\text{exp}}) = \int_{z_{\text{exp}}}^{\infty} (1 + z')^{n/2 - 2} dz' = \frac{(1 + z_{\text{exp}})^{n/2 - 1}}{1 - n/2}$$ $$\tau_0 = \frac{1}{1 - n/2} \approx 1.1357 \text{ units}$$

3. Scale Factor Evolution

In coordinate time, the expansion of the universe is strictly linear (constant velocity, $a \propto t_{\text{coord}}$). However, because proper clocks were ticking faster in the past, proper physical time has accumulated more slowly. Consequently, when mapped to proper physical time $\tau$, the expansion appears to be accelerating:

$$a(\tau) = \left( \frac{\tau}{\tau_0} \right)^{\frac{1}{1 - n/2}} \approx \left( \frac{\tau}{\tau_0} \right)^{1.136}$$

$$\eta(D_C) = e^{\frac{n}{2} \frac{D_C}{D_H}}$$ $$H_{\text{obs}}(D_C) = H_0 e^{(1 + n/2) \frac{D_C}{D_H}} = H_0 (1 + z_{\text{obs}})$$
