1. The Historical Critique: The Factor-of-Two Deflection Deficit

In non-relativistic modified gravity theories (such as classical Modified Newtonian Dynamics, MOND), light deflection has historically been a fatal bottleneck. If light is deflected solely by an effective Newtonian acceleration gradient, the bending angle follows:

$$\alpha_{\text{Newton}} = \frac{2}{c^2} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g_\perp(z) dz = \frac{4 G M}{c^2 b}$$

In massive galaxies and cluster lenses, empirical observations require roughly double this deflection (alpha ~ 8GM / c^2 b). Early phenomenological models attempted to patch this shortfall by multiplying the deflection by an ad-hoc scalar boost factor (pi or 2.0), prompting referees to justly criticize: "Multiplying lensing deflection by an arbitrary scalar factor is a phenomenological fudge without covariant justification."
